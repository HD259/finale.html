Give your carers a clear answer in seconds, and keep your phone for the calls that matter.

Suitable for managers operating community home care services and supporting individuals to remain at home in the comfort of their own homes for as long as possible. Carers receive instant responses on queries, with a permitted, not permitted or Manager review, with a rule behind it.

Paid for by time saved (assumed example below)

Assume 200 home support workers, 20 manager queries a day, 70% routine, 6 minutes each, 25 working days. That is about 35 manager hours a month, roughly €1,050 at an assumed €30 an hour. At an assumed €400 a month (about €2 per worker), the net saving could be about €650 a month.

How people cope today

It is assumed, at the time of posting, that management teams continue to field several phone calls per day from care staff to query whether select tasks are permitted or not. Using this tool is expected to reduce management workload and give home support workers (HSWs) on the front line more consistent response times. Managers would only handle exceptions, reducing the admin burden while maintaining human oversight.

How carers find answers today (assumed): they phone or message a manager, or look through a paper or PDF copy of the policy.

Two real products that cover part of this. I read both pages myself on 28 October 2026.

Access Group: home care software (Ireland site)
theaccessgroup.com/en-ie/health-social-care/sectors/home-care (checked 28 October 2026)

Its page describes a suite for home care agencies: care planning, rostering, electronic medication records, call monitoring and training. It also lists a library of policies and procedures that staff can open in a browser app.

Not seen on that page: a way for a carer to describe a specific request and get a Permitted, Not permitted or Manager review answer.

OnCare: home care system with a policies and procedures product
weareoncare.com (checked 28 October 2026)

Its page describes an all-in-one home care system. The policies and procedures product has 150+ policies, and carers can open them in the mobile app, which the page says works offline. The page lists the policies product at £950.40 plus VAT a year on annual billing.

Not seen on that page: a way for a carer to describe a specific request and get a Permitted, Not permitted or Manager review answer.

Both may have features their pages do not show. What CareScope adds is a quick answer to one specific request, with escalations counted for the manager. That is shown in the demo with sample data only. It does not replace these products.

Tested by someone else

Tested by one person who did not build it. I asked a home-care manager to review the demo. They tested it on their own iPhone 15, on and off for several hours, including in airplane mode after loading it once. It worked fine, and nothing confused them.

What they suggested: add a search bar, and tidy the request list. They pointed out that a carer may always remind a service user of anything, so some reminder requests did not need their own buttons. They also wanted some requests written as the questions carers actually ask.

What I changed:

  • Added a search bar above the request buttons.
  • Removed two requests: reminding about eye or ear drops, and reminding about a pain patch.
  • Reworded five requests as carers’ questions, for example “Can I place tablets on a plate or in an egg cup?” and “Must I document if medication has been taken?”
  • Added “Can the service user travel in my car?”, which shows Not permitted.

One tester, one phone. This is a small test, not a study.
